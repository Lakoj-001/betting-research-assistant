### The basics

**Decimal odds** (e.g. 7.00): how much you get back for every $1 bet, *including* your $1. A $10 bet at 7.00 returns $70 if it wins ($60 profit). If it loses, you lose the $10.

**Favourite / underdog:** the favourite has the low odds (e.g. 1.15) and is expected to win. The underdog has the high odds (e.g. 7.00). A **longshot** is a big underdog.

**Implied chance:** the odds turned into a probability: 1 ÷ odds. Odds of 7.00 imply 1 ÷ 7.00 = 14.3%, or about 1 win in 7.

**Head-to-head (two meanings):** in this app, *head-to-head record* is how two teams have gone against each other. A *head-to-head market* (or "h2h") is a bet on who wins the game, which is the only kind of odds this app shows.

### How bookmakers make money

**Margin** (also "overround" or "vig"): add both teams' implied chances and you get more than 100%, e.g. 105%. The extra 5% is the bookmaker's cut. Their odds are always a little worse than fair, so on average bettors lose.

**Fair odds / fair chance:** the odds with the margin taken out, averaged across other bookmakers. It's the market's best guess at the real chance, not a certainty.

**Betting exchange** (Betfair): people bet against each other instead of against a bookmaker. Prices are usually close to fair, but Betfair takes a commission (about 5%) from winnings, which this app subtracts.

**Same owner:** Neds and Ladbrokes belong to the same company and usually share prices, so they count as one opinion, not two.

### Value and edge

**Price above fair:** a bookmaker paying more than the market's fair odds, e.g. 7.00 when fair odds are 6.67.

**Edge:** how much a price beats fair, on average. Edge = fair chance × odds − 1. A fair chance of 15% (fair odds 1 ÷ 0.15 = 6.67) at odds of 7.00 gives 0.15 × 7.00 − 1 = **+5%**: every $1 bet returns about $1.05 *on average, over many bets*.

**Long run vs. one game (variance):** an edge says nothing about tonight. In the example above, the underdog still loses 85% of the time. Across 100 similar $10 bets you'd expect to end up about $50 ahead, but in any run of 20 you could easily lose 18. Even a real edge comes with long losing streaks.

**Grade (A–F):** this app's confidence in an edge. A bigger edge scores higher. The grade drops a letter when:
- few bookmakers were compared, or they disagree a lot
- the team is a longshot (fair chance under 25%), because bookmakers load extra margin onto underdogs and their "fair" chance is the least reliable

**Stale odds:** odds move as news (like injuries) and money come in. Odds here can be up to an hour old, and an apparent edge may already be gone.

### Words this app won't help with

**Stake:** how much money you bet. This app never suggests stakes.

**Multi / parlay:** several bets combined into one, where all must win. They pay big, but the bookmaker's margin multiplies with each leg, which makes them one of the worst-value bets. This app doesn't build them.

**Chasing losses:** betting more to win back money you've lost. It's the most common way gambling gets out of hand. If you notice it, stop, and see the support services below.

### Stats terms

**Record (e.g. 30-11):** wins, then losses.

**Home / away:** teams usually play better at home (familiar court, crowd, no travel).

**Regular season vs. playoffs:** the regular season is 82 games from October to April. The playoffs are knockout series (best of 7) after that. Last season's playoff games may not reflect current form.
